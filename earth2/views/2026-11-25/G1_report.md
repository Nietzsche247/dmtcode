5. Japan is the swing factor: if the Bank of Japan skips Dec 18, the yen breaks 160, Tokyo intervenes and sells Treasuries, which pushes US long yields up at the worst time. The graph lacks that link. (JUDGMENT)

## 2. ARM: AI FINANCING AND BANKS

### 2.1 Behaviour chain: who is forced, who protects first, cheapest move at each step

Step 1. Holders who can be redeemed or marked move first. Retail credit-fund investors already queue (F-29). Banks with data-center loans protect themselves by selling participations (ING, F-26) and buying credit insurance (Barclays, per Korea JoongAng Daily citing FT, SECONDARY). Cheapest move for a bank: pass the risk on at a small discount. (FACT, SECONDARY; JUDGMENT on cost)

Step 2. The equity window shuts. CoreWeave fell from $60.77 (Nov 11) to $42.57 (Nov 25); ANT is below its IPO price; unlock supply lands Dec 8, Dec 15, Jan 5, Jan 19, Feb 2 and then about 1.7B ANT shares after FY2026 results (F-12, F-14, F-45, F-46). Neoclouds cannot sell stock or unsecured bonds at sane prices (CoreWeave 2031s at 18%, F-23). (FACT)

Step 3. Customers rescue, because it is cheap for them. Meta's contract backs a $4.5B loan at 7 to 7.5% (F-23); about $9B of prepayments is expected (F-22). Meta needs the capacity; backing the loan costs Meta nothing up front. This is why near-term defaults of the big names are less likely than default insurance implies. (FACT, SECONDARY for loan; JUDGMENT for the logic)

Step 4. Oracle defends its investment grade, because a cut to junk would raise the cost of over $100B of debt. S&P is at BBB- with a stable outlook; Moody's affirmed Baa2 in November (F-20). Oracle already chose equity in November (F-19). Its next cheapest moves at the December results: re-phase capex, more stock or equity-linked paper, or push customers to prepay. (FACT, JUDGMENT)

Step 5. OpenAI re-phases. With revenue reportedly short of plan and partners strained, the cheapest move is to push out compute timing, not cancel. Suppliers disclose it as backlog changes (HP04). (BASE RATE: 2001, carriers cancelled or delayed equipment orders at Lucent and Nortel about a year after the March 2000 equity peak. JUDGMENT for OpenAI)

Step 6. Hyperscalers protect their stock prices. In late January they will most likely guide 2027 capex up, with more leasing and partner vehicles, rather than cut. A real cut comes only after AI revenue disappoints. (BASE RATE: telecom capex peaked in 2000 and was cut in 2001; Meta trimmed its 2023 capex guide in Feb 2023, months after its stock fell. JUDGMENT)

Step 7. Defaults land where no big customer will pay to rescue: small neoclouds, miner-hosts, single-asset data-center vehicles. First under $1B (already: Aolani freeze, F-25), then $1B+ in Q2 to Q3 2027. (BASE RATE: after the March 2000 peak, ICG filed in Nov 2000, Winstar in Apr 2001, PSINet in Jun 2001, Global Crossing in Jan 2002. The AI equity peak was about Oct 2026 (F-8, F-13). JUDGMENT)

Step 8. CoreWeave's own test comes when its $9.2B of current recourse debt (F-22) meets a shut equity window. With bonds near 75 cents, a discounted debt exchange is the cheapest move for the company. Rating agencies call that a default. (BASE RATE: 2008 to 09 and 2020 energy distressed exchanges. JUDGMENT: more likely mid-2027 than before March)

Step 9. Banks and insurers admit losses last; accounting lags. (BASE RATE: 2007 to 08; Executive Life seized 1991 after the 1989 to 90 junk break)

Step 10. The Fed does not backstop credit funds while core inflation is above 3% unless banks or money funds are hit. (BASE RATE: the 2020 facilities came with inflation under 2%. JUDGMENT)

## 3. ARM: FED RATES AND JAPAN

### 3.1 Behaviour chain

Step 1. The Fed is forced. Headline PCE 3.8%, core 3.3%, GDP 4.3%, and two hike dissents in October (F-1 to F-4). It hikes Dec 9 to 4.00 to 4.25%. (FACT plus JUDGMENT)

Step 2. The Fed protects its credibility first, then markets. It stops once credit spreads widen enough to tighten conditions for it. (BASE RATE: hiked Dec 19, 2018, paused Jan 4, 2019 after high yield hit 533 bp and the S&P fell about 20%. BASE RATE: 1998, after LTCM, the Fed cut three times. Here inflation is far higher than in 2018, so the pause comes later and needs more pain. JUDGMENT)

Step 3. The Bank of Japan wants to hike (policy rate 1.25%; Reuters poll 86% for Dec) but may wait if markets stay rough. (BASE RATE: after the Aug 2024 carry unwind, Deputy Governor Uchida said the bank would not hike in unstable markets. JUDGMENT: Dec 18 hike about 75%, not 85%)

Step 4. If the BOJ skips, the yen goes through 160. Tokyo's cheapest response is words first, then ¥5T+ of buying, sometimes with the US Treasury (Jul 31). (FACT F-36: interventions came at 160.23 and 163.86.) Japan pays from reserves and sells Treasuries (FACT F-35: holdings fell $66.8B in May and $35.6B in Aug). That pushes US long yields up just as Washington needs buyers. (JUDGMENT)

Step 5. The US Treasury's cheapest response to weak long auctions is more bills, fewer coupons and buybacks, not asking the Fed to buy. (BASE RATE: Treasury leaned on bills in 2024 to 2025. The 30-year cleared at 2.36 cover in November, near the MR3 line of 2.3 (F-40).) Fed bond buying while inflation is above target needs a market break like the 2022 gilt spiral. (BASE RATE: Bank of England, Sept 28, 2022, inflation near 10%)

Step 6. Congress. In the lame duck the cheapest move is a short stopgap into early 2027 (Democrats want it). The risk moves to the stopgap's end date under divided government. (BASE RATE: shutdowns 2013 (16 days), Jan 2018 (3), Dec 2018 to Jan 2019 (35, began in a lame duck), Oct to Nov 2025 (43). JUDGMENT)
