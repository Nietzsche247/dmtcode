The cheapest corporate response to this setting is to keep not hiring, not to fire.

## 1. WHITE-COLLAR WORK

### 1a. Behaviour chain (who moves, in what order, cheapest step first)

1. Firms facing a Fed hike, 3.3% core inflation and a stock correction cut cost the cheapest way: freeze backfills and slow junior hiring. No severance, no WARN notice, no headline. The data already shows this shape (hires rate 3.4%, layoffs 1.0%, claims 189k: G7). JUDGMENT.
2. Juniors absorb it first, but quietly: the Stanford gap rose to 19% (G12) while NY Fed graduate unemployment fell to about 5.2% to 5.6% (G11). New grads take worse jobs or stay in school before they show up as unemployed. JUDGMENT.
3. Firms that do cut now choose the story. Investors still like "AI efficiency"; voters and states now punish it (CT disclosure in force, CA "technology displacement" label from Jan 1, local data-center defeats, G20, G26). The cheapest label is "restructuring" or "economic conditions", which is exactly what Challenger shows (G9). So AI-named cuts fall even if AI-driven substitution rises. This is a naming discount, and it bites W1 and HP13 directly. JUDGMENT.
4. The exception is Big Tech. The correction is an AI-capex correction (G4). The cheapest way for Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta to defend margins is opex cuts in the late-January earnings window, and their investors want proof of AI payback, so they are the most likely to name AI. JUDGMENT.
5. Seat-based software vendors protect themselves before seats fall by repricing to agents and consumption, so reported subscription revenue keeps growing (Workday +12.8%, G15). Seat declines show up late, if ever, in the headline numbers. JUDGMENT.
6. Indian IT is hit earliest because clients cut discretionary outsourced labor first: Infosys guide 1% to 2%, Wipro bookings down 58.6% q/q and headcount down 6,716 (G18, G19). FACT for the numbers; JUDGMENT for the ordering.
7. States respond to named cuts, Congress lags. CT and CA already acted; New York's statute is next in the Jan to June session. JUDGMENT.

BASE RATE (not re-fetched): in 2001 and 2008, white-collar layoffs arrived after the downturn was visible in GDP and claims, not before. In 2001 to 2003, information-sector jobs fell for about three years after the dot-com peak. In the 1990s offshoring backlash and again in 2003 to 2004, state bills on outsourcing disclosure spread only after named cases piled up in the press. The current tape shows neither a downturn nor a pile-up of named cases.

## 2. JOBS AND THE BACKLASH

### 2a. Behaviour chain

1. The backlash went physical and electoral before it went to labor law. Local voters approved dozens of data-center limits on Nov 3, and five newly elected governors (including Texas, Georgia and Ohio) ran on statewide pauses (G26). FACT (SECONDARY).
2. Once sworn in (January 2027), the cheapest move for a governor is an executive order on state permits, exactly as New York (July 14) and Oregon (Oct 9) did, not a statute (G25). JUDGMENT.
3. Utilities and regulators protect ratepayers first by pushing large-load tariffs and interconnection holds onto data centers (Texas PUC/ERCOT hold since Aug 3, G25). JUDGMENT.
4. Builders adapt by moving (Louisiana, Oklahoma were named, G26), going behind the meter, or using federal land. Hyperscalers protect their stock first: they relocate quietly and avoid publicly cutting capacity targets. That makes FC5's settlement (a public cut citing opposition) lag reality. JUDGMENT.
5. Federal preemption has not arrived by statute; the DOJ task force has acted only in Colorado (G22). States keep legislating. JUDGMENT: preemption by statute in the next 12 months is unlikely; litigation is slower than sessions.
6. Chatbot-harm litigation is moving from families to state attorneys general (California and Oregon filings reported, SECONDARY), but FC8 settles only on a verdict or a federal minors' law.

BASE RATE (not re-fetched): the 1990s and early 2000s offshoring backlash produced many state bills but few lasting laws; the 2003 to 2004 wave passed a handful (procurement limits) and faded when the labor market tightened. Data centers are different from offshoring because the object is local, visible and uses local power and water, so the backlash has a physical target. JUDGMENT.
